# Sample Transport Management System

Designed and launched the Sample Transport Management System (STMS) as part of IQHealth — IQLine's modular Hospital Information Management System. The STMS module enables end-to-end tracking of medical samples as they move between collection centers, hospitals, and diagnostic labs, giving operations teams real-time visibility into every handoff in the chain.

This was one of the first modules I took from zero to launch after joining IQLine as Product Design Lead in September 2024. The goal was to design a system that could slot cleanly into the broader IQHealth architecture while solving a very specific, high-stakes operational problem: samples getting lost, delayed, or mis-routed between facilities.

## Project Overview

IQHealth is a modular HIMS built for Indian healthcare facility operations — covering diagnostics, billing, patient management, and inter-facility workflows. The Sample Transport module sits at the intersection of diagnostics and operations: it tracks the physical movement of samples collected at one location and processed at another.

### The Problem Space

In multi-facility diagnostic networks, sample transport is operationally critical but typically untracked beyond paper logs or WhatsApp messages. The existing workflow at client facilities relied on:
- Manual registers at collection centers for dispatch recording
- No visibility between dispatch and receipt
- No structured handoff or chain of custody process
- Delayed turnaround time (TAT) due to untracked transit
- No way to escalate lost or delayed samples before results were due

Operations managers had no dashboard. Lab staff had no ETA. Field runners had no structured check-in flow.

### The Design Objective

Build a transport tracking module that:
- Fits the modular IQHealth architecture without duplicating data from existing LIS/billing modules
- Works for three distinct roles: lab operations, field transport staff, and facility coordinators
- Handles the full lifecycle: collection → dispatch → in-transit → received → exception
- Supports both mobile (for field staff) and desktop (for ops dashboards)
- Keeps the interaction model simple enough for non-technical field staff

## UX Highlights

- **Role-aware interface** — separate views and flows for lab ops, transport runners, and facility coordinators
- **End-to-end status visibility** — real-time sample status from dispatch through receipt
- **Structured handoff model** — digital chain of custody replacing WhatsApp-based coordination
- **Exception handling workflows** — escalation paths for delayed, missing, or rejected samples
- **Mobile-first for field operations** — optimised for quick scan-and-confirm flows on the go
- **Integrated into IQHealth design system** — consistent with the broader HIMS component library

## Key Flows Designed

### Sample Dispatch (Lab / Collection Center)

The dispatch flow allows collection center staff to batch samples for transport, assign them to a runner, and generate a digital manifest. Key decisions:
- Batch-first model to match how labs actually work (multiple samples per trip)
- Manifest generated as a scannable reference, not just a list
- Confirmation screen surfaces sample count and destination before committing

![Samples worklist with tray-side assignment — pending and collected samples staged into the active transport tray](/images/projects/iqhealthsampleswithtray.png)

The collection-side worklist is built around batch-first behaviour. The technician scans or selects samples on the left; the active tray on the right fills in real time, with slot-level visibility into what's already in the batch. Status pills (pending / collected / blood / serum) and a smart-tag column make it possible to triage what belongs in the next shipment without leaving the screen.

### In-Transit Tracking (Field Runner — Mobile)

Designed for speed and low friction. A runner receives a trip assignment, can scan or tap to confirm pickup, and updates status at each checkpoint:
- Minimal text input — status updates are tap-based
- Offline-tolerant: queues updates when connectivity is poor
- Photo capture for sample condition on pickup and delivery

### Receipt & Verification (Receiving Lab)

Receiving staff scan or confirm each sample against the manifest, flag discrepancies, and close the transport chain:
- Mismatch detection flags samples that arrive without dispatch records
- Temperature or condition notes captured at receipt
- Auto-triggers notification to dispatching facility on completion

### Operations Dashboard

The dashboard gives lab managers and coordinators a live view of all active transports, overdue samples, and historical TAT data:
- Status breakdown: pending dispatch / in transit / received / exceptions
- TAT trends per route and per runner
- One-click drill-down into individual transport records

![Shipments dashboard — pipeline of draft, out-for-delivery, and delivered shipments with shipment-detail drawer](/images/projects/iqhealthsampleshipments.png)

The shipments view is the operations team's primary surface. Shipments are grouped by lifecycle stage (draft / out for delivery / delivered) with quick filters and bulk actions, and the detail drawer on the right surfaces sample contents, manifest, and chain-of-custody actions without forcing the operator to navigate away from the queue.

## Design Process

### Discovery

Started with stakeholder interviews at IQLine — product, operations, and the sales team who had direct client feedback. Mapped the current-state workflow at two client facilities to understand where the real breakdowns were happening.

**Key findings:**
- The biggest pain point was not tracking during transit — it was the handoff gaps at dispatch and receipt
- Operations managers spent time chasing runners on WhatsApp for status updates
- Lab staff wanted TAT visibility, not just proof of dispatch
- Field runners needed something faster than a form — any friction meant they'd skip it

### Information Architecture & Role Mapping

Defined three primary roles and their distinct needs before touching any screens. Designed role-specific entry points rather than a single unified flow that would compromise each role's workflow.

Mapped out the data model to align with IQHealth's existing patient and sample entities — the transport module needed to reference samples already created in the LIS module, not duplicate them.

### Wireframing & Prototyping

Built low-fidelity flows for all three roles simultaneously to catch handoff dependencies early. Moved to high-fidelity prototypes for the dispatch and receipt flows first, since those had the most interaction complexity.

Ran walkthrough sessions with the IQLine product team and two client-side ops managers using Figma prototypes before any dev work started.

### Design System Integration

All components were built within the IQHealth design system I was simultaneously developing at IQLine — tokens, form patterns, status indicators, and navigation structures shared with the broader HIMS modules. This kept the transport module visually and functionally consistent with the rest of the platform.

## Impact

The STMS module launched as part of the IQHealth platform rollout at IQLine's client facilities. It replaced the manual paper + WhatsApp tracking workflow with a structured digital chain of custody.

From internal feedback after launch:
- Operations managers reported significantly reduced time spent on status follow-ups
- Lab coordinators gained visibility into expected TAT for the first time
- The exception flagging flow surfaced sample delays earlier, giving labs time to re-collect when needed

## Reflections

This project reinforced the value of designing for handoffs, not just individual screens. The critical UX work wasn't in the dashboard or the mobile app in isolation — it was in how the dispatch confirmation from one party reliably became the pickup record for the next. Getting that data contract right in the design made the dev implementation cleaner too.

Working within a modular HIMS also meant resisting the urge to solve adjacent problems in the same module. The transport system tracks samples in transit — it doesn't try to be an LIS or a billing tool. That constraint kept the interface focused.
