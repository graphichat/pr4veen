# IQHealth — End-to-End HIMS Platform

Leading the UX architecture and product modernisation of IQHealth — IQLine's end-to-end Hospital Information Management System. IQHealth covers the full spectrum of healthcare facility operations: patient registration and OPD workflows, IPD and ward management, diagnostics and lab operations, pharmacy, billing, inter-facility sample transport, and reporting.

This is an ongoing, long-horizon project. I joined IQLine as Product Design Lead in September 2024 with a mandate to modernise the platform's UX, rebuild the design system from scratch, and drive a structural redesign focused on modularity and multi-product interoperability. The work spans product strategy, information architecture, hands-on UX design across modules, design system development, and design process establishment for the team.

## Product Overview

IQHealth serves hospitals, diagnostic centres, polyclinics, and multi-facility healthcare networks across India. The platform handles workflows that span clinical, administrative, and operational functions — often within a single facility session.

### Platform Scope

**Patient & Clinical**
- Patient registration, UHID management, and EMR
- OPD workflows: consultation, prescription, and follow-up scheduling
- IPD: admission, ward allocation, bed management, discharge, and summary
- Clinical notes and encounter documentation

**Diagnostics & Lab**
- Test order management and sample collection
- LIS (Laboratory Information System) — result entry, validation, and report generation
- Radiology and imaging order tracking
- Sample Transport Management (inter-facility — covered separately)

**Pharmacy**
- Formulary and inventory management
- Inpatient medication dispensing
- Outpatient pharmacy billing and dispensing
- Drug interaction alerts and substitution management

**Billing & Finance**
- OPD and IPD billing with package and insurance support
- TPA and cashless claim processing
- Patient ledger and payment tracking
- Financial reporting and MIS exports

**Administration & Operations**
- Staff and roster management
- Appointment scheduling and queue management
- Facility and asset management
- Regulatory reporting (NABH, NMC compliance exports)

**Inter-Facility Operations**
- Sample transport tracking (STMS module)
- Referral management
- Shared patient records across facility network

## The Design Challenge

IQHealth existed before I joined — it had grown feature-by-feature over time, with inconsistencies accumulating across modules. The core challenges I inherited were:

**Architectural fragmentation** — Each module had evolved independently, resulting in inconsistent navigation patterns, duplicate UI components across modules, and workflows that required users to context-switch between disconnected interfaces.

**No design system** — Components were recreated per module by different developers and, where design existed, it was undocumented. There was no shared token set, no component library, and no design-to-dev handoff standard.

**Workflow complexity without clarity** — HIMS workflows are inherently complex. The existing UI had absorbed that complexity without filtering it — screens were dense, action hierarchies were unclear, and users relied on training and habit rather than interface guidance.

**Scalability constraints** — The platform needed to support multi-facility deployments, role-based views, and new module integrations without each addition requiring a full re-think of the existing interface.

## Design Strategy

My approach to modernising IQHealth was sequenced in three layers:

### 1. Design System First

Before redesigning any screens, I built the IQHealth design system from scratch — establishing the foundation that all module redesigns would inherit. This included:

- **Token architecture**: colour, typography, spacing, border, and shadow tokens with semantic naming for light/dark mode support
- **Component library**: 300+ components covering forms, tables, status indicators, navigation patterns, modals, empty states, and data-dense layouts specific to HIMS use cases
- **Patterns and templates**: standardised page layouts, workflow step patterns, and data table configurations that module designs draw from
- **Documentation and handoff standards**: component specs, usage guidelines, and Figma-to-dev handoff conventions aligned with the engineering team's workflow

Building this first meant that each subsequent module redesign contributed to the system rather than diverging from it.

### 2. Structural Redesign — Navigation and IA

Redesigned the global navigation architecture to support:
- Role-aware navigation: clinical staff, billing staff, admin, and lab technicians each have different primary workflows — the navigation reflects this without requiring a separate product per role
- Module coherence: related workflows are grouped and accessible within consistent structural patterns
- Context preservation: users moving between patient context and module functions don't lose where they are in a workflow

### 3. Module-by-Module Workflow Redesign

Systematic redesign of individual modules, prioritised by user impact and development readiness. Each module redesign follows the same process: workflow audit → IA restructure → wireframes → prototype → design review → handoff.

## Key Design Work

### OPD Consultation Workflow

The OPD consultation flow is the highest-frequency workflow in the system — used by doctors for every patient visit. The redesign focused on:
- Reducing the cognitive load of the consultation interface by surfacing only what's relevant to the current encounter stage
- Separating the clinical note-taking surface from order management (investigations, prescriptions) while keeping them contextually linked
- Making patient history and prior visit context accessible without navigating away from the active consultation

### IPD Admission and Ward Management

IPD workflows involve multiple staff roles in sequence — from admission through nursing, pharmacy, and discharge. Key design work:
- Bed availability and allocation interface redesigned for at-a-glance decision-making by admissions staff
- Nursing station view: patient list with live status indicators for pending orders, vitals, and medication schedules
- Discharge checklist and summary generation to reduce documentation time at checkout

### Billing and Insurance

Billing in a HIMS context is complex — packages, item-level billing, insurance TPA coordination, and advance payment management all live in the same workflow. The redesign:
- Introduced a staged billing flow separating order accumulation from finalisation and payment
- TPA/cashless workflow made explicit with clear status tracking for claim submission and approval
- Patient ledger view redesigned for faster cash counter use

### Lab Operations (LIS)

The LIS module handles test requests, sample processing, result entry, and report delivery. Redesigned with:
- Worklist views optimised for lab technicians — batch-friendly, filterable by test type and urgency
- Result entry forms adapted to test type (numeric ranges, text findings, image attachment)
- Report preview and authorisation flow for pathologists before report release
- Turnaround time visibility for operations management

## Design Process at IQLine

One of the less visible but significant parts of this project was establishing a design process where there wasn't a formal one before.

This included:
- **Design review cadence**: weekly design review sessions with product and engineering to align before development starts
- **Feedback and iteration loops**: structured usability walkthroughs with internal subject-matter experts (clinical staff, billing specialists) at key design milestones
- **AI-assisted tooling**: introduced AI tools into the design workflow for faster documentation generation, copy drafting, and design exploration — reducing turnaround on lower-stakes deliverables
- **Design-to-dev handoff standards**: Figma component documentation and annotation conventions that reduced back-and-forth during implementation

## Reflections

Designing an end-to-end HIMS is a different class of problem than designing a single product or feature. The challenge is never just the individual screen — it's ensuring that the design decisions made in one module don't create friction or inconsistency in another, and that the system as a whole is coherent enough for users who move across multiple modules in a single day.

The design system investment paid off quickly. Having shared foundations meant that new module work could move faster, design reviews spent less time on component-level decisions, and the platform started to feel like one product rather than a collection of tools.

The ongoing work is centred on completing the module redesign backlog, expanding the design system to cover edge cases surfaced during implementation, and beginning work on IQHealth's next architectural evolution — supporting multi-facility data federation and advanced analytics reporting.
